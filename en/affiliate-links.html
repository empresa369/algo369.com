Transparency

Affiliate links

Some links on this website may earn us a commission. Here we explain in plain words what that means.

The essentials

What an affiliate link is

An affiliate link is an ordinary link to the website of a shop or a service, with a code that says: “This person comes from Algo369”. If you click on it and buy something, the shop may pay us a commission, that is, a share of the sale price.

So that the shop knows you came from us, it or the programme records the click and may store a cookie in your browser, on its own website. More on this in the privacy policy.

The price you pay does not go up because you use our link. The commission comes out of the shop’s margin, not out of your pocket.

Sometimes a programme gives a discount to people who arrive through an affiliate link. When that is the case, we say so right next to the link.

Our rules

How we recommend

1 Only what we can back up

We recommend something because we have used it or because we can explain why it is good for the purpose. Never because there is a commission for it.

2 The commission doesn’t decide

If there is a cheaper, free or better option without a commission, we say that too. There may be no affiliate link in the text at all.

3 Always labelled

Next to every affiliate link there is the label Affiliate link, visible before you click. A page with such links also has a notice at the top.

4 On social media too

On Instagram and other networks, we write “Ad · Affiliate link” at the start of the text, not hidden at the end after the hashtags.

Who we work with

Programmes we take part in

At the moment we do not yet take part in any affiliate programme, and the website contains no affiliate links.

As soon as we are accepted into a programme, it will appear in this list, with its name and the date.

What we do not promote

We do not place affiliate links to brokers, crypto platforms, investment signals, “copy trading”, betting providers or casinos, even if they pay well.

Why

The basis for these rules

A paid recommendation must be recognisable as such. That is a matter of honesty, and also of law:

  • Switzerland: the Federal Act against Unfair Competition (UCA, SR 241) declares deceptive conduct (Art. 2) and misleading statements (Art. 3 para. 1 let. b) to be unfair. The Swiss Commission for Fair Practices states in its rule B.15 that commercial communication must be clearly recognisable as such, including on blogs and social media accounts.
  • European Union: Directive 2005/29/EC on unfair commercial practices classifies a commercial practice as misleading if it fails to identify its commercial intent (Art. 7(2)). According to the European Commission’s guidance on this directive (2021/C 526/01, section 4.2.6), a percentage of sales made through links also counts as consideration, and the disclosure must be clearly visible.

If you find an affiliate link without a label, let us know at hello@algo369.com. We will correct it.
